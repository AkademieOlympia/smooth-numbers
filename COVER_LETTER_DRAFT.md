Dear Editor,

We submit for your consideration our manuscript "Δr-Dependent Competition in Residue-Class Gap Asymmetries: Geometric Bias vs. Arithmetic Correlations."

## Main Contribution

We identify a universal geometric baseline for residue-class gap asymmetries in prime numbers and isolate a residual arithmetic amplification factor **A(Δr)** whose structure cannot be explained by Bernoulli or Cramér null models alone.

Specifically, we show that prime gap asymmetry can be decomposed as:

```
R_Prime(Δr) = R_Bernoulli(Δr) · A(Δr)
```

where:
- **R_Bernoulli(Δr)** represents the universal geometric short-gap bias present in any sparse point process (proven: (1-p)^(-Δr))
- **A(Δr)** encodes prime-specific arithmetic correlations (empirically determined)

We emphasize that the present work does not provide a theoretical derivation of A(Δr). Rather, it isolates this factor empirically through systematic null model comparison (N=10^6, 20 independent seeds) and proposes it as a quantitative target for future Hardy-Littlewood-type analysis. The robustness of our numerical findings is established through error bars and consistent behavior across multiple moduli (12, 30), though formal significance testing and alternative gap-pair selection remain valuable directions for strengthening the statistical foundation.

## Key Results

1. **Geometric Baseline**: We derive an exact formula for Bernoulli processes: R = (1-p)^(-Δr)

2. **Cramér Overdamping**: The Cramér model universally suppresses asymmetry across all residue distances tested (Δr = 2 to 24)

3. **Regime-Dependent Competition**: Real primes exhibit Δr-dependent behavior with three robust regimes:
   - Twin enhancement: A(2) ≈ 1.06 (N=10^6, 20 seeds)
   - Geometric dominance: A(6) ≈ 0.84
   - Large-distance enhancement: A(24) ≈ 1.18

4. **Empirical Target**: The factor A(Δr) provides a quantitative target for future Hardy-Littlewood k-tuple analysis

## Methodological Approach

This decomposition follows the classical analytic number theory paradigm:
- **Main term**: Universal geometric baseline (proven)
- **Correction term**: Arithmetic signature (empirically isolated)

Rather than attempting to explain prime structure directly, we isolate the arithmetic component as a new mathematical object requiring theoretical derivation.

## Significance

This decomposition follows the classical analytic number theory paradigm of separating main terms from correction terms. Rather than attempting to explain prime structure directly, we isolate the arithmetic component as a new mathematical object requiring theoretical derivation.

Works that isolate empirical signatures often become starting points for theoretical developments. The shift from "Why R_Prime > R_Cramér?" to "What is A(Δr)?" represents progress in problem formulation.

We believe this work provides:
1. A robust empirical benchmark (N=10^6, 20 seeds, systematic Δr-variation)
2. A clean separation of universal and prime-specific effects
3. A concrete quantitative target for Hardy-Littlewood k-tuple analysis

## Conclusion

This work does not claim to explain prime gaps. Instead, it identifies a universal baseline and isolates a prime-specific factor that requires explanation. The most important development during our research was the willingness to revise initial interpretations: our modulo-12 hypothesis of an "intermediate regime" was falsified by systematic modulo-30 testing, leading to the more general Δr-dependent decomposition. This methodological contribution—identifying what needs to be explained rather than prematurely claiming an explanation—may be more valuable than a premature explanatory claim.
